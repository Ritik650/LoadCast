The Idea

Injury is a process,
not an event

01
Most tabular approaches summarise a month into averages. But an athlete who trained steadily and one who ramped hard in the final week can have identical means and completely different risk. Our thesis: the shape of the 30 days carries the signal, and the shape is measurable.
HypothesisTestable

Load outruns adaptation

Overuse injury follows a mismatch between workload accumulation and the body's capacity to adapt. That mismatch leaves fingerprints: rising acute-to-chronic load, falling training variation, autonomic drift, and shrinking recovery.

Prediction if trueFalsifiable

A visible pre-ramp

If the thesis holds, injured athletes should show a measurable upward load trajectory in the observation window that healthy athletes do not — and the steepness of that ramp should encode how soon the breakdown arrives.

VerdictConfirmed

The data agrees

Aligned on true onset, mean load climbs 324 → 411 then falls off a cliff. Observation-window features reach |r| = 0.855 against onset day. Slide 04 shows it.

What makes this approach differentIdeation
1

Scale-free, not population-normalised. Every core feature is a ratio or a slope measured against the athlete's own baseline. One model serves a goalkeeper and a sprinter without per-sport retraining.

2

The scoring function is the spec. We implemented the brief's metric — penalty, baseline, skill — as a validation function before writing a model, then argued every decision against that number.

3

Physiology, not just statistics. Each feature family encodes a published injury mechanism, so importances are readable by a coach and the output names a cause, not just a risk.

Why it matters beyond the leaderboard

A binary label tells a physio nothing actionable. A ranked watchlist with an onset estimate and a named driver — ramp, monotony, RHR drift, sleep debt — changes next week's training plan.

Scope of Round 1

Ideation plus a working end-to-end prototype: 100 features, three model heads, cross-validated against the official metric, and two valid submissions generated. Round 2 turns the idea into a survival model and a staff-facing board.

Problem & Scoring

Three predictions,
one hidden month

02
Days 1–30 are visible; days 31–60 are not. The two timing numbers are graded on every athlete who is truly injured — including the ones we said were fine.
Target AF1

injured_in_risk_window

Binary. Does an onset fall inside days 31–60? Both false alarms and misses cost.

Target BMAE → skill

onset_day_offset

Integer 1–30. Which day the injury begins, scored against a mean-prediction baseline.

Target CMAE → skill

recovery_duration

Days sidelined after onset. Required for every athlete — even those predicted healthy.

The four trapsWhat actually makes this hard
1

Asymmetric penalty. A missed injury books 30 days of error on both timing heads — 3.9× the onset baseline MAE. One miss cannot be repaid by a hundred good timing predictions.

2

Train ships 60 days; test ships 30. The extra month is the answer key, and a leakage trap that would inflate validation and collapse on the leaderboard.

3

Conditional targets, unconditional scoring. Regressors can only learn from injured athletes, yet must fire for everyone.

4

Answers must cohere. A confident risk call with an incoherent onset day is not a usable recommendation.

Metric, implemented literallyvalidation harness
  • Hit (predicted 1, truly 1): MAE against true onset and recovery.
  • Miss (predicted 0, truly 1): fixed PENALTY = 30 on both.
  • Skill = max(0, 1 − MAE_model / MAE_baseline).
  • Baseline = training-set mean onset (7.61 MAE) and mean recovery (3.24 MAE).
Consequence we designed around

The two heads die at different speeds: recovery skill is gone once recall falls below ~99%, onset skill once it falls below ~81%. Nothing short of near-total recall keeps both alive — which is why our optimum ends up degenerate. Slide 08.

Findings that shaped the modelEDA
  • Static risk factors are nearly useless. Injury rate varies only 0.322–0.365 across six sports, and prior-season injuries run the wrong way (0→.355, 1→.344, 2→.322) before a noisy jump at 3 (.492, n = 63). The signal is not demographic.
  • Onset is near-uniform over 1–30, so the mean-prediction baseline is weak (MAE 7.61) and genuinely beatable.
  • Recovery is bell-shaped but unconditioned — its strongest correlate anywhere in 100 features reaches only |r| = 0.058.
  • Class balance is workable at 35% positive; no resampling needed.
Where we looked next

If who the athlete is does not predict injury, then what the athlete did must. That pushed the entire feature effort into temporal dynamics.

Key Insight

Injury announces
itself in advance

04
Averaging daily load across all 1,050 injured athletes, aligned on each athlete's true onset day, exposes the mechanism: a ramp running at least two weeks, then a cliff. This is the pattern the features are built to catch while it is still inside the observation window.
Mean daily load, aligned on onsetn = 1,050
Pre-injury ramp Onset cliff Recovery climb
Separation inside the observation windowdays 1–30 only
  • Acute:chronic ratio 1.051 vs 1.000. Injured athletes end the month accelerating; healthy ones are flat. Small in absolute terms, highly consistent across 3,000 athletes.
  • Onset correlates up to |r| = 0.855 with single observation-window features. The ramp's steepness encodes how far away the breakdown is.
  • Recovery correlates with nothing — best feature |r| = 0.058. We report this rather than pretend otherwise.
The counter-intuitive result

When the injury lands is substantially easier to predict than whether it lands — the inverse of the usual assumption, and the reason our scoring strategy protects timing above all else.

Quality auditWhat we found
  • SedentaryMinutes is constant at 1200 for all 213 K rows — zero variance, and it silently produced four dead features.
  • TrackerDistance equals TotalDistance in 100% of rows — perfectly redundant.
  • TotalSleepRecords is always 1.
  • No nulls and no duplicate athlete-days in the daily or sleep tables.
MissingnessHandled, not dropped
  • weightLogInfo covers 83.5% of athletes, median 3 logs each; the Fat column is 69.4% null.
  • We keep the source and let LightGBM route NaN natively — discarding it would drop a sixth of the cohort's body-composition signal.
  • The RandomForest head, which cannot take NaN, gets a median imputer inside its pipeline.
  • Every other source has 100% athlete coverage.
Pruning108 → 100 features

Zero-variance and exactly-duplicated columns are detected on the training set only and removed from both sets.

  • 7 constant: three SedentaryMinutes_* columns plus sed_slope, rest_days, hours_max, sessions_per_active_day
  • 1 duplicate: active_days
Measured, one variable at a time

Re-running the identical pipeline with --no-prune isolates it: AUC +0.005, F1 +0.010, but recovery skill −0.006 and composite −0.002. We keep it because F1 is an officially scored target and our composite is not.

Feature Engineering

100 features from
sports science

06
Each family encodes a published mechanism of overuse injury — load accumulation, insufficient variation, autonomic drift, recovery debt, schedule congestion — which is why the resulting importances are interpretable to a coach rather than a black box.
Family 01Load & ACWR

Internal load proxy 3×very + 2×fairly + 1×light active minutes, then acute:chronic ratios at 7:28 and 3:21.

  • Weekly means, W4/W1 ratio, largest week-over-week jump
  • Spike days above 1.5× personal median
Family 02Monotony & strain

Mean load ÷ its standard deviation. Grinding the same session daily with no hard/easy variation is a precursor independent of volume.

  • Strain = total load × monotony
  • Personal-baseline z-score of the peak day
Family 03Autonomic

Nocturnal resting HR reconstructed from the 01:00–05:00 band, then its slope across the month. Rising RHR under constant load = non-adaptation.

  • Last-7 vs whole-window delta
  • HR reserve trend, daily max drift
Family 04Recovery

Cumulative sleep shortfall against an 8-hour reference, efficiency (asleep ÷ in bed), nights under six hours, final-week trend against the month.

Family 05Congestion

Longest streak of consecutive training days without rest, sessions per active day, scrimmage-vs-gym mix, dispersion of session start times as a circadian proxy.

Family 06Context & crosses

Sport, position, team, BMI, experience-per-age, prior injuries per season — crossed with load to give load_per_sleep, acwr×prior_inj, rhr_rise×load.

Six mechanisms, one latent — the honest reading

We engineered six independent mechanisms; this dataset contains roughly one. Monotony, ACWR, RHR drift and load slope inter-correlate at |r| = 0.80–0.99, median 0.92 (monotony vs strain: 0.9995), 70 pairs table-wide exceed 0.98, and one principal component holds 92.8% of that block — which is why four unrelated features all land at |r| ≈ 0.855 against onset. Onset MAE by how much of it you use: 7.61 baseline → 3.43 one feature → 2.92 PC1 → 2.64 all 95 (2.61 with the shipped blend).

Final architecturethree heads, one table
  • Head A — LightGBM classifier, 31 leaves, lr 0.03, L2 = 5, early stopping on fold AUC. All 3,000 athletes; categoricals passed natively, categories fixed across train and test.
  • Heads B & C — 0.6 × LightGBM(L1) + 0.4 × RandomForest, fit on the 1,050 injured only. L1 objective deliberately: the metric is MAE, so the loss is the metric.
  • Ensembling — averaged across the five folds; the saved artefact is that ensemble, so inference reproduces the validated numbers exactly.
The narrowness is the finding

Logistic regression reaches 0.761 vs LightGBM's 0.767. Not “great features” — slide 06: the predictors are near-collinear, so there is one signal and every model finds it.

Read the comparison honestly

Benchmark rows use the unpruned 108-feature table at fixed 600 trees; the final row adds pruning and early stopping. Native cats lose 0.010 AUC at fixed depth — early stopping recovers it.

Scoring Strategy

The penalty inverts
the usual trade-off

08
Chasing the best F1 is the obvious move, and it is the wrong one. Because a missed injury costs 30 days on both timing heads, recall is not a preference here — it is a budget constraint, and we can compute exactly where it binds.
Threshold sweep, out-of-fold3,000 athletes
Onset skill score F1 score
The arithmeticwhy 0.21 is a cliff

Measured, not illustrated: at threshold 0.21 recall is 0.805, and the scored onset MAE is 7.643 against a 7.615 baseline. Skill is already exactly zero there — a fifth of the injuries missed is enough to erase every correct timing prediction.

Moving from the F1 peak at 0.37 down to 0.06 costs 0.138 F1 and buys back 0.657 onset skill plus 0.055 recovery skill. A five-to-one return in the units the leaderboard adds up.

Impact & Round 2

From leaderboard
to touchline

10
A ranked risk board is more useful to a coach than a binary label. The same model that produces the submission produces a daily watchlist with the reason attached — and every feature it uses is one a coach can act on.
Practicalityruns today

Deployable now

  • End-to-end in ~6 min on a laptop CPU; no GPU, no external services
  • pandas + LightGBM + scikit-learn only
  • Inference for a new athlete is a single 100-column row
Scalabilitylinear

Scales by athlete

  • Feature build is O(n) and per-athlete independent — trivially parallel across a squad or a league
  • Rolling windows recompute incrementally; a nightly job replaces the batch
  • Saved ensemble is a few MB — deployable on-prem at a club
Impactdecisions it changes

Who acts on it

  • Rank the squad by risk, then deload the top decile before the ramp completes
  • Onset to ±2.6 days lets medical staff pre-book scans and plan rotation
  • Attribution names the cause: ramp, monotony, RHR drift or sleep debt
Round 2 build planprioritised
1

Discrete-time hazard model. Onset day is a time-to-event target. A per-day hazard over days 31–60 makes Tasks A and B the same model — the survival curve yields both the probability and the expected day, so consistency is guaranteed rather than patched on.

2

Quantile regression for recovery. Given the weak signal, an 80% interval beats a point estimate.

3

Model the latent directly. Correlation pruning is tested and rejected — it costs up to 0.011 AUC and never improves onset MAE; best case, unchanged. The open lever is the reverse: estimate the shared ramp factor explicitly, plus a sequence encoder over the raw 30×24 grid.

Also queued

Per-sport calibration · SHAP · group-aware CV · seed ensembling. Ask the organisers: are Tasks A and B combined or scored separately? That decides the primary file.

  • Pipeline — build_features, train_model, predict
  • Evidence — eda_profile, benchmark_models, collinearity, --no-prune ablation
